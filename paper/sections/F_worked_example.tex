\section{Worked Example}
\label{app:worked-example}

The example is small enough to check by hand.

\paragraph{Setting.}
$\Vocab = \{\textit{the}, \textit{cat}, \textit{sat}, \textit{mat}\}$, two labels which we read
as $\{\textsc{n}, \textsc{v}\}$, one channel, $\lamZ = \lamH = \lamW = 1$, a
distance-insensitive arc score ($\clip = 0$), one inner round per observed slot and $\rho = 2$
rounds in predictive mode. The factors are
%
\[
  \Sfac = \begin{pmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;
  \Tfac{} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\!,
\]
\[
  \bfac = (1,0,0,0), \qquad \rfac{} = (0, 1.5).
\]
%
The prefix is \textit{the cat sat}, and the model is asked for the word at slot 4.

\paragraph{Observed mode.}
Each slot initialises from its own unary, $\QZ{}^{(0)} = \sigma(\Sfac_{w,\cdot})$, giving
$(.953,.047)$, $(.982,.018)$ and $(.018,.982)$. One round of the head and label updates against
the frozen prefix then gives the filtering marginals
%
\[
  \qbar{1} = (.818,.182), \quad \qbar{2} = (.957,.043),
\]
\vspace{-1.2em}
\[
  \qbar{3} = (.006,.994).
\]
%
The third word is labelled as the verb. Contracting these beliefs into arc scores by
\Cref{eq:contract} gives the cache that the next slot reads,
$\Bfac{}_{1,\cdot} = (.365,.818)$, $\Bfac{}_{2,\cdot} = (.085,.957)$ and
$\Bfac{}_{3,\cdot} = (1.987,.006)$. Nothing else of the prefix survives.

\paragraph{Predictive mode.}
The word variable is unclamped, so it initialises from its own unary factor,
$\QW{}^{(0)} = \sigma(\bfac) = (.475,.175,.175,.175)$, which gives the query
$\tilde{s} = (1.063,-1.063)$ and $\QZ{}^{(0)} = (.893,.107)$. The query is the
\textsc{[mask]} embedding, produced by the same initialisation rule as every other variable. Two
rounds of the inner loop over the head domain $\{\textsc{root}, 1, 2, 3\}$ give
%
\begin{align*}
  F^{(1)} &= (.160,.413,.178,1.776) \\
  \QH{}{(1)} &= (.120,.154,.122,.604) \\
  \QZ{}^{(1)} &= (.951,.049) \\[3pt]
  F^{(2)} &= (.074,.387,.128,1.890) \\
  \QH{}{(2)} &= (.104,.143,.110,.642) \\
  \QZ{}^{(2)} &= (.956,.044)
\end{align*}
%
The slot attends mostly to position 3, the verb, which is the head a noun object should choose.
The word update is the output layer. It gives the logits $(2.368, 1.824, -1.824, 1.824)$ and
%
\[
  \hat{p}(\Wv{4}) = (.460,\, .267,\, .007,\, .267)
\]
%
over $(\textit{the},\textit{cat},\textit{sat},\textit{mat})$. The verb is almost excluded, and
\textit{mat} and \textit{cat} tie because nothing in this two-label toy separates two nouns.

\paragraph{Observing the word.}
Clamping $\Wv{4} = \textit{mat}$ and running the same slot in observed mode sharpens the
label belief (entropy $0.180 \to 0.040$ nats) and raises the attention on the verb from $.642$
to $.663$. As the construction predicts, observing the word makes the slot more certain of both
its label and its head.

\paragraph{The exact readout on the same slot.}
With the same parameters, \Cref{eq:exact-readout} gives
$\hat{p}(\Wv{4}) = (.365,.215,.206,.215)$. This is flatter, and it no longer almost excludes the
verb. The mean-field readout commits to one soft label and reads the vocabulary off it, while
the exact readout mixes over labels, so a word compatible with an unlikely label keeps more mass.
\Cref{sec:exp3} compares the two readouts after training.

\section{What the Latent Variables Learn}
\label{app:interpretability}

The label and head variables are meant to carry syntactic categories and dependency
attachments. We probe trained models to see what they carry.

\paragraph{Words that each label explains.}
The word--label factor is the only place where a word touches the graph, so the words a label
explains are those with the largest $\Sfac_{w,a}$. Raw scores favour words with large rows, so
we rank by the contrastive score $\Sfac_{w,a} - \max_{a' \neq a}\Sfac_{w,a'}$. Of $\nlab = 32$
labels, several are recognisably syntactic:

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
label & top words by contrastive score \\
\midrule
$z{=}6$  & \textit{reconsider condemn leave lend hire notify restore} \\
$z{=}30$ & \textit{prefers manages chooses earns requires bears helps} \\
$z{=}13$ & \textit{to affects between from until thus indeed besides} \\
$z{=}19$ & \textit{controlled punishable bolstered funded headed owned} \\
$z{=}20$ & \textit{henderson cook jr.\ fisher sr.\ robertson generale} \\
$z{=}26$ & \textit{pence marks miles equaling tons} \\
$z{=}22$ & \textit{speculation deficits view sums notion evidence} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
In order, these are bare infinitives, third-person singular present verbs, prepositions and
subordinators, past participles, person names, units of measure and abstract nouns. No
supervision produced these categories; they come from maximising the likelihood through a
$32$-value bottleneck. About a third of the labels resist any reading we can offer, as
\citet{wu2023probabilistic} report for the encoder.

\paragraph{Where the head posterior looks.}
The head variable is the model's dependency attachment, and here the configuration matters
most. We probed two trained models: the damped configuration ($\nlab=32$, original temperature,
damped) and the standardised configuration ($\nlab=96$, $\krank=12$, standardised temperature,
undamped; \Cref{sec:exp-width}).

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & damped & standardised \\
\midrule
\textsc{root} mass                & $0.486$ & $0.277$ \\
mean attachment distance          & $1.05$  & $11.06$ \\
mass at distance 1                & $0.976$ & $0.327$ \\
mass beyond distance 3            & $0.001$ & $0.497$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The damped model has not learned attachment. It puts $0.976$ of its positional mass at distance
one and almost none beyond three: a bigram attachment with large root mass. The standardised
model has a mean attachment distance of $11.1$, against $17.0$ for uniform attention over the
same prefixes. It is $5.2$ times uniform at distance one, yet still places half its mass beyond
distance three (\Cref{fig:attachment}). This is a preference for nearby heads, not a collapse
onto the previous word.

The two models give opposite answers to the same probe; what separates them is the head
temperature and the arc-score rank. An interpretability result for this model class says little
without the parameterisation that produced it.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\columnwidth]{figures/fig_attachment.png}
\caption{Head-posterior attachment distance in the standardised configuration ($\nlab=96$,
$\krank=12$) against uniform attention over the same prefixes. The posterior is $5.2$ times
uniform at distance one, yet half its mass lies beyond distance three; mean distance $11.1$
against $17.0$. Distances $1$--$3$ have their own arc scores ($\clip=3$); longer ones share
one.}
\label{fig:attachment}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Predictive against observed beliefs.}
We measure the KL divergence, in nats, from a slot's predictive label belief to its observed
one. We expect it to be small on
predictable tokens and to grow with the surprisal of the observed word. Over $4{,}032$ slots per
model the expected direction appears in both models, and its strength separates them as the
attachment probe does. In the standardised configuration the correlation is $r = 0.47$, with a
mean divergence of $4.07$ on the most predictable quartile of tokens against $6.56$ on the most
surprising. In the damped configuration it is $r = 0.16$, with $1.98$ against $2.58$. The
expectation concerns the direction only, so we read nothing into the magnitudes beyond this:
the parameterisation decides how much of the predicted structure is there to find.

\paragraph{Width.}
The label lexicon above comes from a model with $\nlab=32$, where each label has enough words to
be readable. At $\nlab=96$ the same probe finds fewer clean categories and more fragments.
Adverbs (\textit{hastily, mildly, extremely, widely}), gerunds (\textit{completing, entering,
joining, contemplating}), units (\textit{bottle, par, pound, capita, bushel}) and time nouns
(\textit{century, decade, phase, 1970s}) survive, but most labels resist reading.
Interpretability and capacity pull in opposite directions along the width axis, and we found no
width at which both are best.
