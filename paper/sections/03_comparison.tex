\section{Comparison with Causal Transformers}
\label{sec:comparison}

\citet{wu2023probabilistic} show that MFVI in their model has the computation graph of a
transformer encoder. The match is closer for the causal model: the factor of two from
symmetrisation disappears, $\Tfac{c}$ need not be symmetric because the arcs are directed, and
excluding self-attachment ($j=i$) is harmless because the re-injected unary plays the residual
role.

\subsection{Tensor form}
\label{sec:tensor-form}

Collect the label beliefs of the prefix into $\bar{Q} \in \R^{\seqlen \times \nlab}$ with rows
$\qbar{j}$, and write $Q_Z^{(t)} \in \R^{\seqlen\times\nlab}$ for the beliefs after iteration
$t$ (here $t$ indexes iterations, not positions).
With $\Tfac{c} = \Ufac{c}\Vfac{c}{}^{\top}$ and $\Ufac{c},\Vfac{c} \in \R^{\nlab\times\krank}$,
\Cref{eq:update-H,eq:update-Z} become, for channel $c$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tensor}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{F}^{(c)} &= \tfrac{1}{\lamH}\big(Q_Z^{(t-1)}\Ufac{c}\big)\big(\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}\big)^{\!\top}
      + M, \\
  A^{(c)} &= \softmax_{\text{rows}}\big(\mathcal{F}^{(c)}\big), \\
  \mathcal{G} &= \textstyle\sum_c A^{(c)}\, \bar{Q}\Vfac{c}\Ufac{c}{}^{\top}, \\
  Q_Z^{(t)} &= \softmax_{\text{rows}}\big(\tfrac{1}{\lamZ}(\Sfac_{w,\cdot} + \mathcal{G})\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where $M$ is the strict lower-triangular mask with the \textsc{root} column prepended.
$Q_Z^{(t-1)}\Ufac{c}$ is the query projection, $\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}$ the key projection, $\lamH$ the
attention temperature and $\mathcal{G}$ the attention output.

\subsection{The update vs.\ masked self-attention}
\label{sec:vs-attention}

\paragraph{Projections, residual and normalisation.}
$\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}$ appears twice in \Cref{eq:tensor}, as the key and as the value: both roles are
the same message through the same arc factor. There is no output projection $W^O$ either; the
energy sums over channels, so their messages are summed. There is no residual stream and no
layer normalisation. The unary $\Sfac_{w_i,\cdot}$, added
again at every iteration, plays the residual role, and the simplex keeps activations bounded. Wu
and Tu control the parameters with an $L_2$ penalty on the arc scores.

\paragraph{The temperature.}
\label{sec:temperature}
Layer normalisation holds a transformer's queries and keys at a roughly constant scale, so one
temperature $1/\sqrt{d_{\text{head}}}$ serves throughout training. Here the query
$\QZ{i}$ and the key source $\qbar{j}$ are probability vectors, whose Euclidean norm ranges over
$[\nlab^{-1/2}, 1]$ from uniform to one-hot, so the scale of the head logit
$\langle \QZ{i}, \Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot}\rangle$ can change by a factor of up to $\nlab$ during
training. Wu and Tu set $\lamH = 1/\nlab$ (the \emph{original temperature}). With arc scores of
scale $\sigma_T$, this gives a scaled head logit of $O(\sigma_T)$ at the near-uniform beliefs of
initialisation but $O(\nlab\,\sigma_T)$ once the beliefs sharpen, an error that grows linearly
with the width. We therefore predict a
positive feedback loop $\qbar{j} \to \Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot} \to \mathcal{G} \to \qbar{j}$ whose gain
grows with $\nlab$, so that any damping of the label update must get stronger as the model gets
wider.

We test two repairs. The \emph{belief-norm temperature} $\lamH\|\QZ{i}\|_2\sqrt{\nlab}$ equals
$\lamH$ at the uniform belief and stays inside the model. The \emph{standardised temperature}
centres the head logits over $\Dep{i}$, scales them to unit root mean square and multiplies them
by a gain $g$ (default $1$). It makes attention invariant to the scale of the arc scores, but it
is a normalisation outside the update equations.

\subsection{The readout vs.\ a language-model head}
\label{sec:cmp-readout}

The encoder has no output layer. Let $L$ be the matrix whose rows
are the log-conditionals $\log p(\cdot \mid \text{context})$ over the vocabulary for a fixed
set of contexts.

\begin{proposition}[Readout expressivity]
\label{prop:readout}
\emph{(i)} Under the mean-field readout $\ell = \bfac + \Sfac\QZ{t}$ with
$\QZ{t}\in\Delta^{\nlab-1}$, the rows of $L$ lie in an affine subspace of dimension at most
$\nlab$. \emph{(ii)} Under a linear--softmax head of width $\dmodel$ they lie in an affine
subspace of dimension at most $\dmodel+1$. \emph{(iii)} The exact readout of \Cref{eq:exact-readout} is not an
affine family: unless the component distributions $p(\cdot\mid a)\propto e^{\bfac + \Sfac_{\cdot,a}}$
are all identical, $\readout{} \mapsto \log\hat p(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in its parameters and its
image is a curved $(\nlab{-}1)$-dimensional manifold not contained in the subspace of (i).
\end{proposition}

The mean-field readout therefore has the softmax bottleneck of \citet{yang2018breaking}, the
same as a transformer's output head at $\nlab = \dmodel$. The exact readout is weaker than a full
mixture of softmaxes, but it lies outside the linear family at no parameter cost. The prefix
reaches the future as one point of $\Delta^{\nlab-1}$ per position, where a transformer's cache
holds one point of $\R^{\dmodel}$.

\Cref{fig:computation-graphs} shows one layer of each model. The causal PT differs from the
transformer in ten ways, each of which can be switched separately in the \emph{switch ladder}
(\Cref{sec:exp-switches}): parameters
shared across iterations (\texttt{weight\_sharing}), no feed-forward block (\texttt{ffn}), no
output projection (\texttt{attn\_out\_proj}), keys tied to values (\texttt{attn\_value}), no
query projection (\texttt{attn\_query\_proj}), no residual stream (\texttt{residual}), no layer
normalisation (\texttt{norm}), a simplex-constrained state (\texttt{state}), relative clipped
positions (\texttt{position}), and a mixture readout over labels in place of a linear head
(\texttt{readout}).

A looped transformer \citep{dehghani2019universal,giannou2023looped,yang2024looped} shares
weights across depth, as the causal PT does, but has no syntactic structure. Comparing it with the transformer isolates
weight sharing, and comparing the causal PT with it isolates structure.
