\section{Comparison with Causal Transformers}
\label{sec:comparison}

% This section is the analogue of Wu & Tu Section 3 ("Comparison with Transformers"), which is
% the LARGEST section of their paper -- about 2.4 pages, as long as their experiments. It had
% no counterpart in the first outline of this paper, and that was the biggest structural gap:
% in this format the conceptual comparison carries as much weight as the numbers, and it is
% also where the Looped baseline of Section 4 gets motivated rather than asserted.

\citet{wu2023probabilistic} show that MFVI in the encoder model reproduces the computation graph
of a transformer encoder, with the posterior over dependency heads playing the role of
self-attention. We ask the same question of the causal model. The correspondence turns out to be
\emph{closer} than in the encoder --- the factor of two from symmetrisation disappears, and no
symmetry assumption on the arc scores is needed --- and where it stops holding is where
\Cref{sec:experiments} looks. This section identifies ten independent differences from a causal
transformer and singles out the one that is not merely a difference but a mis-calibration:
the temperature of an attention whose query is a probability vector.

\subsection{Tensor form}
\label{sec:tensor-form}

Collect the label beliefs of the prefix into $\bar{Q} \in \R^{\seqlen \times \nlab}$ with rows
$\qbar{j}$, and write $Q_Z^{(t)} \in \R^{\seqlen\times\nlab}$ for the beliefs at iteration $t$.
With $\Tfac{c} = \Ufac{c}\Vfac{c}{}^{\top}$ and $\Ufac{c},\Vfac{c} \in \R^{\nlab\times\krank}$,
\Cref{eq:update-H,eq:update-Z} become, for channel $c$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tensor}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{F}^{(c)} &= \tfrac{1}{\lamH}\big(Q_Z^{(t-1)}\Ufac{c}\big)\big(\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}\big)^{\!\top}
      + M, \\
  A^{(c)} &= \softmax_{\text{rows}}\big(\mathcal{F}^{(c)}\big), \\
  \mathcal{G} &= \textstyle\sum_c A^{(c)}\, \bar{Q}\Vfac{c}\Ufac{c}{}^{\top}, \\
  Q_Z^{(t)} &= \softmax_{\text{rows}}\big(\tfrac{1}{\lamZ}(\Sfac_{w,\cdot} + \mathcal{G})\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where $M$ is the strict lower-triangular mask with the \textsc{root} column prepended. Read
against a transformer layer, $Q_Z^{(t-1)}\Ufac{c}$ is the query projection, $\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}$ is
the key projection, $\lamH$ is the attention temperature, and $\mathcal{G}$ is the attention
output. Four structural differences are visible in \Cref{eq:tensor} itself and we take them in
turn.

\subsection{The single-channel update vs.\ masked self-attention}
\label{sec:vs-attention}

\paragraph{Keys are values.}
$\bar{Q}\Vfac{c}$ appears twice in \Cref{eq:tensor}: once as the key against which the query is
scored and once as the value that is mixed. A transformer has independent $W^K$ and $W^V$. This
is not an economy measure; it is forced, because both roles are the \emph{same message} through
the \emph{same} arc factor --- the score $\Bfac{c}_{j,a}$ that decides how much position $j$ is
attended to is the score that position $j$ then contributes. Similarly there is no output
projection $W^O$: channel messages are summed, because the energy sums over channels, and a
learned mixing matrix would be a map with no factor behind it.

\paragraph{The residual is the unary, and the state is a distribution.}
There is no residual stream and no layer normalisation. What plays the residual role is the
re-injection of $\Sfac_{w_i,\cdot}$ at every iteration, and what plays the normalisation role is
the simplex constraint: $Q_Z^{(t)}$ is a probability vector by construction, so activations
cannot blow up at fixed parameters. Only the \emph{parameters} can grow, and the original paper's
control for that is an explicit $L_2$ penalty on the arc scores.

\paragraph{The query is a distribution, and the temperature is calibrated for one point of its
range.}
\label{sec:temperature}
This difference has no counterpart in the encoder discussion and it is, empirically, the one
that matters most. A transformer's query and key are unconstrained vectors whose scale is held
near a constant by layer normalisation, which is why a single temperature $1/\sqrt{d_{\text{head}}}$
serves at every point of training. Here the query $\QZ{i}$ and the key source $\qbar{j}$ are
\emph{probability vectors}, and a distribution on $\nlab$ labels has Euclidean norm anywhere in
$[\nlab^{-1/2}, 1]$: a $\sqrt{\nlab}$ dynamic range, from uniform to one-hot, and the model
traverses it during training. Since the head logit is $\langle \QZ{i}, \Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot}\rangle$
and $\Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot}$ inherits the same range from the key side, the logit scale moves by up
to a factor $\nlab$ between the two extremes.

\citet{wu2023probabilistic} set $\lamH = 1/\nlab$. At initialisation both beliefs are near
uniform, so $\mathcal{F}^{(c)} \sim \sigma_T/\nlab$ and dividing by $1/\nlab$ leaves an
$O(\sigma_T)$ logit: the choice is exactly right \emph{there}. Once training sharpens the
beliefs, $\mathcal{F}^{(c)} \sim \sigma_T$ and the same division leaves $O(\nlab\,\sigma_T)$. The
temperature is calibrated for a limit the model leaves within a few hundred steps, and the
error grows linearly in the width. This predicts a specific pathology --- a positive feedback
loop $\qbar{j} \to \Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot} \to \mathcal{G} \to \qbar{j}$ whose gain increases with $\nlab$ --- and
therefore that any damping of the label update must be strengthened as the model is widened.
\Cref{sec:exp-width} tests this. The collapse it predicts is
found, and found with its signature rather than only its symptom; the accompanying claim about
$\alpha^\star(\nlab)$ is bounded there rather than measured, for a reason given at that point.
The same section tests the repair the argument suggests: a temperature
$\lamH \| \QZ{i}\|_2 \sqrt{\nlab}$, which equals
$\lamH$ exactly at the uniform belief --- so it agrees with the source's choice at
initialisation and differs only where the source's calibration does not reach.

\subsection{The readout vs.\ a language-model head}
\label{sec:cmp-readout}

The encoder has no output layer, so this comparison is new. Fix a set of contexts and let $A$
be the matrix whose rows are the log-conditionals $\log p(\cdot \mid \text{context})$ over the
vocabulary.

\begin{proposition}[Readout expressivity]
\label{prop:readout}
\emph{(i)} Under the mean-field readout $\ell = \bfac + \Sfac\QZ{t}$ with
$\QZ{t}\in\Delta^{\nlab-1}$, the rows of $A$ lie in an affine subspace of dimension at most
$\nlab$. \emph{(ii)} Under a linear--softmax head of width $m$ they lie in an affine subspace of
dimension at most $m+1$. \emph{(iii)} The exact readout of \Cref{eq:exact-readout} is not an
affine family: unless the component distributions $p(\cdot\mid a)\propto e^{\bfac + \Sfac_{\cdot,a}}$
are all identical, $\readout{} \mapsto \log\hat p(\cdot)$ is nonlinear in its parameters and its
image is a curved $(\nlab{-}1)$-dimensional manifold not contained in the subspace of (i).
\end{proposition}

The consequence should be stated bluntly, because it corrects a natural first reading of the
model. \textbf{The mean-field readout's bottleneck is the classical softmax bottleneck}
\citep{yang2018breaking}, \emph{in identical kind and degree} to a baseline transformer's own
output head at $\nlab = d_{\text{model}}$. The comparison unit is $\nlab \leftrightarrow
d_{\text{model}}$, not $\nlab \leftrightarrow$ anything smaller, and parity of the readout
requires only $\nlab \approx d_{\text{model}}$. The exact readout is a different family
altogether: a mixture over $\nlab$ fixed component softmaxes with context-dependent weights ---
weaker than a full mixture-of-softmaxes head, whose components are themselves
context-dependent, but strictly outside the linear family at zero parameter cost.

Two further bottlenecks are often conflated with this one and are worth separating.
The \emph{chain} bottleneck: $\Wv{t} \perp w_{\prefix{t}} \mid z_{\prefix{t}}$ compresses the
prefix into one point of $\Delta^{\nlab-1}$ per position, consumed linearly by the future
through \Cref{eq:contract}. A transformer's KV cache is one point of $\R^{d_{\text{model}}}$ per
position, consumed linearly through $W^K$ and $W^V$: the same structure, with the per-position
width $\nlab{-}1$ against $d_{\text{model}}$, and the genuine difference being the simplex
geometry --- bounded, non-negative, summing to one. And the \emph{dynamics} bottleneck: with the
pipe width matched, the risk is not the pipe but the pump, that is, whether attention-only
iterations with tied parameters and simplex-constrained states can deposit as much usable
information into those $\nlab$ numbers as a transformer stack deposits into
$d_{\text{model}}$. We regard the third as the real one, and \Cref{sec:exp-switches} is designed
to separate the three empirically rather than argue about them.

\Cref{fig:computation-graphs} places one layer of each model side by side. Reading it from the
transformer to the causal PT gives ten independent differences, and this decomposition is not
merely expository: \Cref{sec:exp-switches} implements each as a switch and measures what it
costs, so that ``the causal PT is worse than a transformer'' becomes a statement about
identified components rather than about architectures as wholes.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Parameters shared across iterations.} The property a Looped transformer
        \citep{dehghani2019universal,giannou2023looped,yang2024looped} also has, and the reason
        a comparison against a standard decoder alone cannot attribute any difference to
        structure.
  \item \textbf{No feed-forward structure.} Inherited from the encoder model. The in-graph
        substitute --- the global variables of \Cref{sec:variants} --- is measured rather than
        assumed in \Cref{sec:exp-ablations}.
  \item \textbf{No output projection and keys tied to values} (\Cref{sec:vs-attention}).
  \item \textbf{No query projection}: the query is the evolving belief itself.
  \item \textbf{No residual stream and no layer normalisation}; the unary is re-injected and
        the simplex bounds the state instead.
  \item \textbf{Relative, clipped positions} rather than a learned absolute embedding.
  \item \textbf{The output is a posterior, not a projection}: no parameter exists outside the
        factor graph (\Cref{sec:output}), and the readout is a mixture rather than a linear map
        (\Cref{sec:cmp-readout}).
\end{itemize}
