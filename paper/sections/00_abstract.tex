The probabilistic transformer of \citet{wu2023probabilistic} derives a transformer encoder from
mean-field inference in a conditional random field over latent syntactic labels and dependency
heads. We derive its autoregressive counterpart. Because causality, a single global energy and
contextual dependence cannot hold together, we use a directed chain of conditional random fields
in which the prefix enters each step as a frozen condition. Treating the next word as a latent
variable of the same graph gives a language-model head with no parameter outside the graph, with
tied embeddings and a closed-form mixture-of-softmaxes readout. At matched non-embedding budget
on the Penn Treebank, its perplexity is $\MBMinRatioGpt$ to $\MBMaxRatioGpt$ times that of a
causal transformer. Much of its sensitivity to width comes from a miscalibrated attention
temperature; correcting it with a normalisation outside the factor graph roughly doubles the
usable label-set size and transfers to a second corpus without retuning. On the range where all
three models are measured, the scaling exponents are $\ExpTriTf$ for a causal transformer,
$\ExpTriLp$ for a looped transformer and $\ExpTriLow$ for our model, so most of its shallower
slope already appears with weight sharing alone.
