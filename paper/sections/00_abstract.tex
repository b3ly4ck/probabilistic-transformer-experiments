The Probabilistic Transformer \citep{wu2023probabilistic} models contextual word
representations as posterior inference in a conditional random field over dependency
structure, and recovers a transformer encoder as the computation graph of mean-field
variational inference. It is, however, a masked encoder: no causal autoregressive version
exists. We give one.

We first show that causality, a single global energy function and contextuality cannot hold
simultaneously, and resolve the conflict with a directed chain of \emph{conditional} CRFs in
which the prefix enters each step as a frozen condition, so that the anti-causal term is never
formed rather than removed after the fact. We then obtain a language-model head without
introducing any parameter outside the factor graph, by promoting the word variable from
observed to latent: the existing word--label factor is simply unclamped. Tied input and output
embeddings, an output bias equal to the word unary term, and a single model with two
conditioning patterns all follow as consequences rather than design choices. Because the
per-position subgraph is a star, and hence a tree, the vocabulary readout admits an exact
sum-product solution --- a mixture of softmaxes computed by one causal
\texttt{logcumsumexp} scan --- alongside the mean-field one.
