The probabilistic transformer of \citet{wu2023probabilistic} models contextual word
representation as posterior inference in a conditional random field over dependency structure,
and its mean-field inference procedure turns out to resemble a transformer encoder. That model is
an encoder only. We derive its autoregressive counterpart. Causality, a single global energy and
contextuality cannot hold together, so we replace the single field by a directed chain of
conditional random fields in which the prefix enters each step as a frozen condition and the
anti-causal term is never formed. Promoting the word from an observed constant to a latent
variable then yields a language-model head with no parameter outside the factor graph; tied
embeddings, the output bias and a mixture-of-softmaxes readout follow as consequences, the last
in closed form because the per-position subgraph is a tree. Empirically we find that the model's
sensitivity to its own width is not diffuse but has a mechanism in the calibration of one
temperature, and that correcting it roughly doubles the usable width and makes the model's
scaling curve orderly. Comparing fitted scaling exponents pair by pair on the range each pair
shares, the ordering is the same every time --- a standard transformer is steeper than a looped
one, and a looped one steeper than the causal PT --- which locates most of the model's shallow
slope in weight sharing and the remainder in the syntactic structure itself.
