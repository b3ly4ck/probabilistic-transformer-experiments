\subsection{Tasks and Datasets}
\label{sec:tasks}

We train language models on the Penn Treebank \citep{marcus1993building} in the standard
word-level preprocessing (10{,}000 types; 929{,}589 / 73{,}760 / 82{,}430 tokens) and, as a
transfer test only, on WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017pointer} (33{,}278 types; 2{,}088{,}628 /
217{,}646 / 245{,}569 tokens). Both are read through one code path, with an \texttt{<eos>} token
per line and fixed 64-token blocks cut from the joined stream, so that a difference between the
two corpora cannot be a difference between two tokenisers (\Cref{app:datasets}). The reference
every number is read against is the maximum-likelihood unigram of the training split, scored on
the identical token set: \textbf{688.8} on PTB and \textbf{964.8} on WikiText-2.

\textbf{Every hyperparameter is selected on PTB and transferred to WikiText-2 unchanged.} This
follows the advice of \citet{kuang2026scaling}, whose method for transferring hyperparameters
across model sizes is explicitly not claimed to hold when the corpus changes as well; a
WikiText-2 number produced by a fresh grid search would say nothing about transfer, which is
the property under test.

We deliberately do not use WikiText-103. \citet{wu2023probabilistic} report that the encoder
model underperforms transformers beyond roughly $10^5$ sentences, suspected to be the absence
of a feed-forward structure; running there would test a known failure mode of the encoder
rather than the causal extension.

\subsection{Settings}
\label{sec:settings}

One training loop is shared by every model in this paper, and model-specific behaviour is
reached only through a three-method interface (the loss on a scored token set, an $L_2$
regulariser on the ternary scores which a transformer returns as zero, and a parameter count).
A change that helped one model and not the others would void the comparison, so there is
nothing in the loop that can branch on model type. AdamW, $\beta=(0.9,0.999)$, weight decay
$1.4\times10^{-6}$, gradient clipping at 1.0, 100 warmup steps then cosine decay to $0.1\times$,
$L_2$ on the arc scores $5\times10^{-4}$ (all from \citealp{wu2023probabilistic}, Table 2),
batches of 16 blocks of 64 tokens, 15{,}000 steps. Dropout is 0 for every model: its placement
inside the update equations of a probabilistic transformer is unspecified by the source and any
choice would cross the ``no maps'' constraint of \Cref{sec:variants}, so the baselines run
without it too.

\paragraph{Scored tokens.}
The causal PT emits $\text{logits}[t]$ predicting $w_t$ from $w_{<t}$, including $w_0$ from
\textsc{root} alone; a conventionally trained decoder emits $n-1$ predictions. We wrap the
baseline so that both answer $p(w_t \mid w_{<t})$ and drop slot 0 from every average, which
makes the two token sets identical rather than merely comparable; the baseline's loss asserts
that slot 0 is never scored. Evaluation is deterministic: fixed non-overlapping blocks in
corpus order over the whole split.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection.}
Every model is scored at its best-validation checkpoint, chosen inside the shared loop, and
test perplexity is measured on that same checkpoint. This matters at this scale: PTB has under
a million training tokens, so 15{,}000 steps is 16.5 epochs and the larger baselines reach
their validation optimum long before the end --- a 1.2M-parameter transformer at step 5{,}000
against 15{,}000 for the causal PT. Scoring every model at the last step would grade some of
them past their optimum, and scoring each at its own best step is the standard protocol.

\paragraph{Learning rate.}
Each baseline point is the better of two learning rates ($10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-3}$), and
each rung of the switch ladder of \Cref{sec:exp-switches} the best of three. The causal PT
runs at $2\times10^{-2}$, twenty times the value in the source's Table 2; that this is
necessary, and that it interacts with the label-set size, is itself a finding
(\Cref{sec:exp-width}). A comparison in which one model is tuned and the other is not measures
the tuning.

\paragraph{Two parameter axes, both reported.}
Tying is forced (\Cref{sec:output}), so almost all of the causal PT's parameters sit in
$\Sfac \in \R^{|\Vocab|\times\nlab}$: at $\nlab = 32$ that is 330{,}000 embedding parameters
against 16{,}448 for everything else. A transformer at $d_{\text{model}} = 32$ carries 322{,}048
and 50{,}880. Reporting only the non-embedding count flatters the causal PT by a large factor;
reporting only the total hides that its computational parameters are few. We plot both, and
state which axis any given claim is made on.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Correctness of the implementation.}
\label{sec:exp-correctness}
Two checks test the derivation rather than the code and belong here; the rest are in
\Cref{app:validation}. The mean-field free energy is non-increasing along the inner loop on a
toy graph, and the check carries a mutation test --- a sign-flipped head update fails it while
every shape, normalisation and causality check passes unchanged. And on a single slot the graph
is a tree, so \Cref{eq:exact-readout} must agree with brute-force enumeration of the slot joint:
it does, to $10^{-12}$ in float64, including with a global variable folded in as an extra leaf,
and the \texttt{logcumsumexp} scan agrees with slot-by-slot assembly for
$\clip \in \{0,1,2,4,9\}$. The worked example of the output-mechanism note reproduces digit for
digit. \TODO{state the factored-label brute-force agreement once exp7 lands}

\paragraph{A methodological finding first: variance.}
\label{sec:exp-repro}
A configuration recorded at validation perplexity 430.04 in our own earlier work was re-run
here on the same code, the same seed and the same data order, and landed at 644.98. Three arms
separate the possible causes and they agree to every printed digit --- the pre-refactor source
through its original driver, the current source through that driver, and the current source
through the sweep runner --- so neither the implementation nor the harness is at fault. Two
further facts complete the picture, and together they are more useful than either alone.

The first is hardware. A cell of an unrelated grid happened to run the same configuration on the
same GPU \emph{model} as the original, and reproduced its entire twelve-point validation trace
to every printed digit, ending at $430.0$; the same configuration on the other device traces
$711.6, 640.3, 686.0, \dots$ and ends at $645.0$. The two already differ at the first evaluation
in the third significant figure ($711.70$ against $711.61$), so the split occurs within the first
500 steps and is then amplified to 215 perplexity. Nothing differs but the order of
floating-point reductions.

The second is the seed. Over three seeds on one device the undamped configuration gives
$610.9 / 408.8 / 367.5$, that is $462.4 \pm 130.2$: \textbf{a standard deviation of 28\% of its
own mean}. The earlier figure sits comfortably inside that range.

These are one phenomenon seen twice. In this configuration any perturbation at the last bit ---
a different reduction order, a different seed --- moves the outcome by hundreds of perplexity.
The earlier number was therefore both exactly reproducible on the hardware that produced it and
not a measurement of anything.

The same three seeds with the label update damped ($\alpha_Z = 0.25$) give $322.0 \pm 0.65$ ---
\textbf{0.2\%}, two orders of magnitude tighter. We draw two conclusions and apply them
throughout. No single-seed number from the undamped region is reported as a result here, and
comparisons in that region from the earlier work are withdrawn rather than repeated: a
difference of 43 perplexity against a spread of 130 is not a measurement. And the interventions
this paper is about should be judged on \emph{variance} as much as on the mean. Damping was
previously reported as an improvement from 641.5 to 248.3; it is at least as much an
improvement in reproducibility, and reproducibility is what lets any later comparison mean
anything at all. Every headline number below is a three-seed mean with its spread, and every
grid cell carries a count of seeds that failed to learn, because a mean taken over a mixture of
learned and collapsed runs measures neither.

\paragraph{Scaling curves.}
\label{sec:exp-scaling}
\Cref{fig:scaling} draws validation perplexity against parameter count for all three models,
each point the best of its learning-rate search at a 15{,}000-step budget with
validation-selected checkpoints. Two features of the \emph{baselines} shape everything after.
First, the transformer's own curve is flat above roughly $2\times10^5$ non-embedding
parameters: 129.2 at $d_{\text{model}}=64$ against 124.7 at 160 and 125.3 at 256, with training
perplexity falling from 68.5 to 10.2 over the same range. On a corpus of 929{,}589 tokens the
baseline stops being parameter-limited and starts being data-limited, so a comparison run only
at the top of that range compares two models in a regime where one of them has stopped
improving. Second, at matched \emph{non-embedding} budget the looped transformer is
\emph{better} than the standard one below about $5\times10^4$ parameters (164.7 at 12{,}768
against 195.2 at 13{,}152) and worse above it (135.7 at 198{,}528 against 129.2 at 200{,}064).
At matched \emph{total} parameters the standard transformer wins everywhere. This was written
into the experiment's falsification criterion before it ran, and it revises the framing of
\Cref{tab:three-point}: at this scale weight sharing is not a handicap that the causal PT pays
for structure, it is a saving --- which makes the causal-PT-versus-looped comparison the
sharper one rather than the more forgiving one.

Against those curves the causal PT sits above, and the interesting question is whether it also
sits \emph{parallel}. Fitting a power law to each Pareto envelope over its own measured range
gives $-0.086$ for the causal transformer, $-0.087$ for the looped one and $-0.082$ for the
causal PT with its head temperature corrected, which invites the reading that the correction has
restored the model's scaling exponent to a transformer's. \textbf{That reading is wrong, and the
way it is wrong is worth stating.} The baselines' ranges extend to $1.2\times10^{6}$
non-embedding parameters, well into the regime where this corpus and not the parameter count is
the binding constraint, and those flat points drag their fitted slopes down. Restricted to the
range where all three models are actually measured
($4\times10^{3}$ to $1.5\times10^{5}$), the exponents are $-0.175$ for the transformer, $-0.107$
for the looped transformer and $-0.082$ for the causal PT. \textbf{Per parameter, the causal PT
gains less than either baseline.}

What the correction does buy is measured against the model's own control rather than against the
baselines: the exponent improves from $-0.061$ to $-0.082$ and, more importantly, the ladder
stops being erratic --- the control's seed standard deviation reaches $160.7$ at $\nlab = 64$
where the corrected ladder's is $4.2$. At its best measured point the causal PT reaches
$219.8 \pm 4.2$ at $\nlab = 64$ (65{,}664 non-embedding parameters), against $144.8$ for a
transformer at $50{,}880$ and $133.9$ at $113{,}184$ --- a factor of $1.52$ against the smaller
bracket and $1.64$ against the larger, where the same comparison in our earlier work gave
$2.17$.

\paragraph{Width transfer, and why the source temperature fails.}
\label{sec:exp-width}
\Cref{sec:temperature} predicted that Wu \& Tu's head temperature $\lamH = 1/\nlab$, being
calibrated for the uniform-belief limit, would make the label update's feedback loop worse as
the model is widened, so that any damping of that update must be strengthened with $\nlab$.
\Cref{fig:width}(a) is that prediction measured: the argmin of the damping step $\alpha_Z$
falls from $0.25$ at $\nlab=16$ to $0.125$ at $\nlab=24$ and $\nlab=64$, and at
$\nlab \in \{32, 96, 128\}$ the undamped model sits exactly on the unigram baseline with the
collapse signature the mechanism predicts --- the message runs away (msg/unary $1.7 \to 16.7$)
and the label beliefs then go flat (sharpness $1.09 \to 5.57 \to 1.34$, prefix-ablation KL
exactly zero).

The consequence is visible in the ladders of \Cref{tab:main}. Carrying the constants tuned at
$\nlab = 32$ across the width axis --- which is what one does in the absence of a rule ---
reproduces our earlier record at that width ($253.0 \pm 6.0$ against $250.7 \pm 2.3$) and then
degrades, to $287.4 \pm 52.5$ at $\nlab=48$ and $470.0 \pm 160.7$ at $\nlab=64$. The means matter
less than the standard deviations: the ladder does not gently worsen, it becomes unpredictable.

\Cref{fig:mechanism} shows why, within a run, and it separates two failures that a
final perplexity conflates: at $\nlab = 96$ the beliefs never leave the uniform point at all,
while at $\nlab = 32$ they sharpen to $5.5$, hold for two thousand steps and then collapse back
to uniform. Both end at the unigram baseline; only the second is the runaway the mechanism
predicts, and only a within-run trace tells them apart.

\Cref{fig:width}(b) is the repair. Standardising the head logits over the head domain before
the softmax --- so that the attention pattern is invariant to the overall scale of the arc
scores, and only their relative pattern selects --- revives widths that are entirely dead under
the source temperature, \emph{with no damping at all}: at $\alpha_Z = 1$, $\nlab = 32$ goes from
700.7 to $282.2 \pm 6.6$ over three seeds and $\nlab = 64$ from a collapse to $254.0 \pm 1.9$.
Run as a full ladder at the baselines' budget it reaches $229.8 \pm 8.5$ at $\nlab = 48$ and
$219.8 \pm 4.2$ at $\nlab = 64$, against $287.4 \pm 52.5$ and $470.0 \pm 160.7$ for the control.

\textbf{The gain transfers; the width limit does not disappear.} Sweeping the standardisation
gain over $\{1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ at four widths with three seeds each, the argmin is $1$ at
every width, and the working range is the same $[1,2]$ at every width (\Cref{tab:gain}) --- which is what the
mechanism predicts, since after standardisation the attention entropy depends on the gain and on
the length of the head domain and not on $\nlab$ at all. Above the range the failure is sharp and
reproducible rather than noisy: at $\nlab=32$, gain $3$ collapses on $3/3$ seeds
($700.5 \pm 0.7$). But the correction does not remove the ceiling, it moves it: at
$\nlab \in \{96, 128\}$ the rule ladder becomes unstable again ($372 \pm 277$, $509 \pm 187$,
with one collapsed seed each). The usable width roughly doubles; it does not become unbounded,
and we say so rather than reporting only the widths where it works.

\textbf{Learning-rate transfer is not what changes.} We also ran the diagnostic that maximal
update parametrisation is judged by \citep{yang2022tensor} and that \citet{kuang2026scaling}
apply to the encoder: validation perplexity against learning rate at four widths, under both
temperatures. The argmin moves by one step of a $2\times$ grid over an $8\times$ range of width
in \emph{both} arms ($2\times10^{-2} \to 10^{-2}$ under the source temperature,
$4\times10^{-2} \to 2\times10^{-2}$ under the standardised one; \Cref{tab:lr}). So the two results are about
different things, and we do not claim ours as a substitute for theirs: what the temperature fixes
is not where the optimal learning rate sits, but whether the model learns at all at a given
width.

\textbf{The Kruskal rank inverts the configuration.} The arc scores are decomposed as
$\Tfac{c} = \Ufac{c}\Vfac{c}{}^{\!\top}$ with $\Ufac{c},\Vfac{c} \in \R^{\nlab\times\krank}$, and
every configuration in this literature sets $\krank = \nlab$, which is the most expensive setting
of the decomposition. It is also not the best one: at $\nlab = 96$, $\krank = 48$ gives
$242.6 \pm 3.3$ against $\krank = 96$'s $275.6 \pm 27.1$ --- better perplexity at half the arc
parameters and a fifth of the seed variance --- and $\krank = 12$ gives $257.8 \pm 4.5$ at
18{,}624 non-embedding parameters, eight times smaller and still ahead of the best $\nlab = 32$
configuration at a comparable budget. The reason is structural: the arc score reaches inference
only contracted against a belief, $\Bfac{c}_{j,a} = \E_{\qbar{j}}[\Tfac{c}_{a,\cdot}]$, so what
is usable is its rank on the affine hull of the reachable beliefs (\Cref{tab:rank}), and those are measurably
concentrated. \TODO{the low-rank ladder at full budget}

\textbf{We report what this repair costs, because it is not free.} A query-dependent temperature
is still a temperature, and $\lamH$ is a free per-variable message weight in the source, so
dividing by $\|\QZ{i}\|_2\sqrt{\nlab}$ --- which is $1$ exactly at the uniform belief --- stays
inside the family of choices the construction already makes. Standardising the logits does not:
it is a normalisation applied to the messages, outside the update equations, and it forfeits the
property that every update is the centred gradient of a fixed per-slot free energy. It is, in
other words, precisely the kind of operation \Cref{sec:variants} lists as a tripwire, and it is
the one that works. We regard that as a finding rather than an embarrassment --- it prices one
specific consequence of the factor-graph constraint, and it is the same price a transformer pays
knowingly when it puts a layer normalisation in front of its attention. The variational reading
survives for the content stream and the readout; it is the head-update temperature alone that
steps outside.
