\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Tasks and Datasets}
\label{sec:tasks}

We train language models on the Penn Treebank (PTB; \citealp{marcus1993building}) and use
WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017pointer} as a transfer test only (\Cref{app:datasets}). Two
references are computed on the same scored tokens: the unigram of the training split ($688.8$ on
PTB, $964.8$ on WikiText-2) and an add-one bigram ($445.2$ and $692.5$).

\subsection{Settings}
\label{sec:settings}

One training loop is shared by all models (\Cref{app:datasets}). As in Wu and Tu's Table~2, we
use AdamW with $\beta=(0.9,0.999)$, weight decay $1.4\times10^{-6}$, gradient clipping at $1.0$,
$100$ warm-up steps, cosine decay to $0.1\times$, and an $L_2$ penalty of $5\times10^{-4}$ on the
arc scores. Unless stated otherwise, every model trains for $15{,}000$ steps on batches of $16$
blocks of $64$ tokens, without dropout; the width, gain, learning-rate, rank and initialisation
grids use $6{,}000$ steps. All models are scored on the same tokens at their best-validation checkpoint. Test perplexity at that checkpoint is $5$ to $9\,\%$ below
validation for every model in \Cref{tab:fullrank,tab:wt2} and orders the models the same way.

The baselines are nanoGPT decoders \citep{karpathy2022nanogpt} with four layers and four heads;
the looped transformer applies one such block four times. Each baseline point is the better of
learning rates $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-3}$. The causal PT uses $\nchan=2$, $\niter=3$,
$\clip=3$ and a learning rate of $2\times10^{-2}$, twenty times Wu and Tu's
(\Cref{app:datasets}). Its label update can be damped with a step $\alpha_Z\le1$ (Wu and Tu's
Appendix~B.1). The scaling ladders run undamped under the standardised temperature at gain $1$.
The \emph{damped configuration} (original temperature, $\alpha_Z=0.25$, $\nlab=\krank=32$) is
the base of the constants, depth and readout sweeps. All causal PT numbers use the mean-field readout except in \Cref{sec:exp3}; we did
not retrain the scaling, width, rank and switch grids under the exact readout. With tying,
almost all of the causal PT's parameters are in $\Sfac$ (at $\nlab=32$, $330{,}000$ embedding
against $16{,}448$ other parameters), so we report non-embedding and total counts separately. A
seed \emph{collapses} if it finishes above $95\,\%$ of the unigram perplexity. Collapsed seeds
are counted and left out of the mean, except in \Cref{tab:fullrank}, whose means include them.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsubsection{Correctness and reproducibility}
\label{sec:exp-correctness}
\label{sec:exp-repro}
On a single slot the exact readout matches brute-force enumeration to $6.7\times10^{-16}$; the
free energy never increases along the inner loop, a test that catches a sign-flipped head update
missed by every other check; and changing token $t+1$ leaves the logits at step $t$ bitwise
unchanged (\Cref{app:validation}).

The undamped configuration at the original temperature ($\nlab=16$, $6{,}000$ steps) is highly
sensitive to last-bit perturbations: with the same code, seed and data order it ends at $430.0$
on one GPU model and at $645.0$ on another. Across three seeds on one device it gives
$\ReproUndampedMean \pm \ReproUndampedSd$ (\ReproUndampedPct\,\% of the mean); damping the label
update ($\alpha_Z = 0.25$) reduces this to $\ReproDampedMean \pm \ReproDampedSd$,
\ReproSdRatio{} times tighter (\Cref{app:res-repro}). We therefore report three-seed means with
their spread and a count of collapsed seeds.

\subsubsection{Scaling and matched budget}
\label{sec:exp-scaling}
The transformer stops improving above about $2\times10^5$ non-embedding parameters, where it is
limited by the size of PTB (\Cref{fig:scaling}). The looped transformer beats it at matched
non-embedding budget below about $5\times10^4$ parameters (\Cref{app:grid-exponents}). On
the range where all three models are
measured (\ExpTriLo--\ExpTriHi{} non-embedding parameters), the exponents are $\ExpTriTf$ for the
transformer, $\ExpTriLp$ for the looped transformer and $\ExpTriLow$ for the low-rank causal PT
(\Cref{tab:exponents-trio}). The looped model lies between the other two, closer to the causal
PT, so most of the slope difference already appears with weight sharing alone; the rest comes
from the other nine differences of \Cref{sec:full-comparison}. The fits rest on four, three and
eight points, so only this ordering is robust; pairwise fits, including the full-rank causal PT,
give the same ordering in all five pairs (\Cref{tab:exponents-full}).

At matched non-embedding budget (\Cref{tab:main}) the gap widens with budget. The causal PT's
perplexity is \MBFirstRatioGpt{} times the transformer's at \MBFirstNonemb{} parameters, the
smallest budget with a measured transformer, and \MBLastRatioGpt{} times at \MBLastNonemb{};
against the looped transformer the ratio runs from \MBFirstRatioLooped{} to
\MBLastRatioLooped. The best causal PT reaches $\PTbestVal \pm \PTbestSd$ at
$\nlab=\PTbestLab$, $\krank=\PTbestRank$. These numbers use the standardised temperature, which
is not part of the factor graph (see Limitations).

\subsubsection{Width and the head temperature}
\label{sec:exp-width}
Under the original temperature, damping is necessary and the need for it does not lift as
$\nlab$ grows, consistent with the prediction of \Cref{sec:temperature}
(\Cref{fig:width-alpha}). Undamped, the model ends on the unigram at $\nlab\in\{32,96,128\}$,
and removing the prefix changes its prediction by a KL divergence below $10^{-8}$. The best
damping step is the smallest we swept, $\alpha_Z=0.125$, at five of seven widths, so the grid
bounds the optimal step but does not locate it (\Cref{app:grid-gain}).

The standardised temperature revives dead widths without damping: at gain $1$ and three seeds,
$\nlab=32$ improves from $700.7$ to $282.2\pm6.6$ and $\nlab=64$ reaches $254.0\pm1.9$
(\Cref{tab:gain}, and \Cref{fig:width} for the other rules). At the full budget
it gives $219.8\pm4.2$ at $\nlab=64$, where the damped control gives $470.0\pm160.7$
(\Cref{tab:fullrank}). The correction roughly doubles the usable width but does not remove the
limit: at $\nlab\in\{96,128\}$ the full-rank ladder is unstable again ($372\pm277$ and
$509\pm187$, means that include one collapsed seed each). The best learning rate moves by at
most one grid step over an eightfold range of width under either temperature (\Cref{tab:lr};
one seed per cell),
so the temperature seems to decide whether the model trains at all, a separate question from the
learning-rate transfer of \citet{kuang2026scaling}.

\paragraph{Rank.}
Rank equal to width is not the best choice: at $\nlab=96$, $\krank=48$ gives $242.6\pm3.3$
against $275.6\pm27.1$ at $\krank=96$ (\Cref{tab:rank}), although at
$\nlab=32$ full rank is best. Wu and Tu's rule $\krank=\min(64,\nlab)$ gives $\krank=\nlab$ for
every $\nlab\le64$ (see \Cref{app:grid-rank} for a likely reason).
The low-rank ladder trains stably up to $\nlab=\LadderTopLab$, $\krank=\LadderTopRank$
($\LadderTopVal\pm\LadderTopSd$), but at $\nlab=256$, $\krank=64$ one seed in three still
collapses: the ceiling moves without disappearing.

\subsubsection{The switch ladder}
\label{sec:exp-switches}
The switch ladder measures the cost of each difference of \Cref{sec:full-comparison} in two
directions (\Cref{tab:switches}; positive values mean the causal PT's version is worse). S1
gives a tuned causal transformer one property of the causal PT; S2 restores one transformer
property in a model with all ten properties of the causal PT (the all-PT model). In S1, value
tying ($-0.6$), relative positions ($-1.0$) and dropping the query projection ($+1.9$) are nearly
free, and so is layer normalisation once each switch has its own learning rate; the
feed-forward block ($+15.1$) and weight sharing ($+12.5$) cost more, and with the mixture
readout a transformer does not train at all ($686.8$). In S2, restoring nine of the ten
transformer properties makes the all-PT model worse; only value tying is neutral ($+0.7$).
With one seed per cell and the best of four learning rates per switch, differences of a point or
two cannot be told from zero. Every S1 optimum is interior, but the all-PT model's best learning
rate lies at the top of its grid, so the S2 magnitudes are lower bounds (\Cref{app:grid-switches}).

\subsubsection{Constants, global variables and depth}
\label{sec:exp-knobs}
\label{sec:exp-depth}
\label{sec:exp-ablations}
Under the standardised temperature at $\nlab=32$, a global variable has no effect ($243.2\pm5.3$
without it, $242.5$ to $254.1$ with it across three initialisation scales, with and without the
shared $L_2$ penalty); at $\nlab=64$ it changes which configurations are stable without making
the model stable. Its posterior stays near uniform (entropy at least $0.93$ of the maximum), so
it sends a near-constant label prior. A semi-orthogonal initialisation of $\Bglob$ lowers the
seed standard deviation from $10.2$ (Gaussian) to $0.6$ (\Cref{app:grid-globalhead}).

The constants and depth sweeps use the damped configuration and one seed per cell; its seed
standard deviation is about $6$ (\Cref{app:res-knobs}). The $L_2$ coefficient has an interior optimum near Wu and
Tu's value ($5\times10^{-4}$ and $5\times10^{-3}$ are within noise), and of the clips tried ($0$,
$1$, $3$, $7$) only $1$ and $3$ train. More iterations do not help: $\niter=1,2,3,5$ give
$241.3$, $246.5$, $248.3$ and $297.0$. The first three differ by about one standard deviation;
$\niter=5$ costs $56$, about nine. $\niter=8$ ($275.3$) breaks the trend, which one seed cannot
resolve. The other experiments keep Wu and Tu's $\niter=3$.

\subsubsection{Exact vs.\ mean-field readout, and factored labels}
\label{sec:exp3}
\label{sec:exp-factored}
Swapping in the exact readout at evaluation, on mean-field-trained weights, multiplies
perplexity by \SwapMildestRatio{} to \SwapWorstRatio{} across \SwapNconfigs{} configurations
(\Cref{app:res-readout}), so we train under each readout. With one seed each, the exact readout
then reaches $348.8$ against $248.3$ at $\nlab=32$, and $409.5$ against $315.5$ at $\nlab=16$.
It is the richer family (\Cref{prop:readout}), yet it trains worse. We conjecture that its
log-sum-exp over the head domain acts like a soft maximum and sends most of the gradient to the
best-scoring position.

At a fixed arc budget of $8{,}256$ parameters, going from $\ncomp=1$ to $\ncomp=2$ also doubles
the label-set size, and the mean-field model improves from $283.8\pm8.3$ ($\nlab=16$) to
$230.6\pm4.4$ ($\nlab=32$); $\ncomp=4$ adds nothing. As predicted in \Cref{sec:variants}, the
exact readout's penalty falls with $\ncomp$ on both budgets we tested, from $+52.2$ at $\ncomp=1$
to $-3.5$ at $\ncomp=4$ on the smaller one (three seeds per cell; \Cref{tab:penalty}). The sign
change in the last cell is within a seed spread, so the claim is the trend. At a fixed
$\nlab=32$ the exact readout keeps improving while $94\,\%$ of the arc parameters are removed,
but it never beats the mean-field readout's best cell ($230.6$ at $\ncomp=2$;
\Cref{app:grid-factored}).

\subsubsection{Transfer to WikiText-2}
\label{sec:exp-wt2}
With nothing retuned, the standardised temperature beats the original one on WikiText-2 at both
widths: $\WtStdNarrow\pm\WtStdNarrowSd$ against $\WtSrcNarrow\pm\WtSrcNarrowSd$ at $\nlab=32$,
and $\WtStdWide\pm\WtStdWideSd$ against $\WtSrcWide\pm\WtSrcWideSd$ at $\nlab=64$
(\Cref{tab:wt2}). It also gains more from width, while the original temperature's change is
within its seed spread, which suggests the correction is not tuned to PTB. The gap to the
baselines persists (\Cref{app:grid-wt2}).
