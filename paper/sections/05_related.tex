\paragraph{Probabilistic transformers and their scaling.}
Our model is the autoregressive counterpart of \citet{wu2023probabilistic}, who derive a
transformer \emph{encoder} from mean-field inference in a dependency CRF and name a decoder as
an open extension. \citet{kuang2026scaling} take the same encoder in the other direction,
transporting maximal update parametrisation \citep{yang2022tensor} to it so that
hyperparameters tuned on a small model transfer to a large one, and report that the
probabilistic transformer then \emph{outperforms} a standard transformer at matched parameter
budget on masked language modelling up to 0.4B parameters. Two things follow for the present
paper. Their result removes the assumption --- inherited from the 2023 paper's own discussion
and from our first framing --- that a probabilistic transformer must lose on perplexity; the
gap it reports at small scale is a tuning artefact, not a property of the model class. And the
width problem we analyse in \Cref{sec:temperature} is the decoder's version of the problem they
solve for the encoder, with a different mechanism: theirs is a rescaling of parameters and
learning rates, ours is a mis-calibration of the attention \emph{temperature} that arises
specifically because the query of this attention is a probability vector. The two are
complementary rather than alternative, and \Cref{sec:exp-width} measures them against each
other.

\paragraph{Weight sharing and looped models.}
Applying one block repeatedly with shared weights is the defining property of Universal
Transformers \citep{dehghani2019universal} and of the looped transformers studied by
\citet{giannou2023looped} and \citet{yang2024looped}. A probabilistic transformer has that
property by construction, because every MFVI iteration is inference in the \emph{same} model;
untying the iterations would make each one inference in a different model. This is why the
looped transformer, and not only a standard decoder, is a necessary baseline here
(\Cref{tab:three-point}).

\paragraph{The softmax bottleneck.}
\citet{yang2018breaking} show that a linear--softmax output head confines the log-conditionals
to a low-dimensional affine family, and propose a mixture of softmaxes as the repair. Our exact
readout (\Cref{eq:exact-readout}) is a mixture of softmaxes that arrives from a different
direction: it is what sum--product inference on the slot tree \emph{is}, with the mixture
components fixed by the word--label factor and the weights supplied by the prefix, and it costs
no parameters. \Cref{prop:readout} states the consequence and \Cref{sec:exp3} measures it.

\paragraph{Syntax in neural models.}
The broader line that \citet{wu2023probabilistic} situate themselves in --- structured latent
variables, syntactic inductive bias, and analyses reading dependency structure out of attention
--- applies here unchanged, and we do not repeat it.
