\section{Additional Variants}
\label{app:variants}

% Mirrors Wu & Tu Appendix B, where the variants not used in the main experiments live --
% including the B.3 global variables that Section 2.6 promotes to a measured ablation here.

\Cref{sec:variants} gives each of these variants a sentence. This appendix gives the
construction, the checks it passes, and --- where the choice was decided by a measurement
rather than by an argument --- the number that decided it.

\subsection{Global variables}
\label{app:globals}

\citet{wu2023probabilistic} introduce global variables in their Appendix B.3 as the in-graph
substitute for the feed-forward structure their model lacks, in three designs: \emph{all-dep},
in which a word's head may be one of $\nglob$ observed global feature variables;
\emph{dep-split}, in which each channel carries a separate global head; and
\emph{single-split}, in which each position carries exactly one global head, shared by all
channels. We take the third, which is the design their own discussion identifies as the closest
to a transformer's computation, and the one \Cref{sec:variants} refers to.

In the causal model the construction is unchanged, because it is position-local. Slot $t$ gains
one variable $\Gv{t}$ ranging over $\nglob$ values and one binary factor
$\phi_b(\Gv{t}{=}k, \Zv{t}{=}a) = \exp(\Bglob_{k,a})$ with a single score matrix
$\Bglob \in \R^{\nglob \times \nlab}$. There is no channel axis --- that is what distinguishes
single-split from dep-split --- so the message enters once per iteration rather than once per
channel, and the per-slot energy of \Cref{eq:slot-energy} gains the one term
$-\sum_{k,a} \QG{t}(k)\,\QZ{t}(a)\,\Bglob_{k,a}$.

Three things stay true, and they are the reasons the variant is admissible here at all. The
slot remains a tree: $\Gv{t}$ is one more leaf off $\Zv{t}$, joining $\Wv{t}$ and the
$\nchan$ head variables, so sum--product on the slot is still exact and
\Cref{prop:exact-readout} still applies. Causality is untouched: $\Gv{t}$ reads no prefix
quantity and no future one, so $\qbar{j}$ remains a function of $w_{1:j}$ alone. And the cost
is $O(\nglob\nlab)$ per position, independent of the sequence length.

The mean-field updates gain exactly one pair of messages,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-update}
\begin{aligned}
  \QG{t}(k) &\propto \exp\Big(\tfrac{1}{\lamG}\textstyle\sum_a \QZ{t}(a)\,\Bglob_{k,a}\Big),\\
  \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{glob}}_{t}(a) &= \textstyle\sum_k \QG{t}(k)\,\Bglob_{k,a},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
the second line being the message that joins the arc messages inside the bracket of
\Cref{eq:update-Z}. Composed, the two are the operator $\sigma(q\Bglob^{\!\top}\!/\lamG)\Bglob$ acting on the label belief
$q$ --- the global feature update that \citet{wu2023probabilistic} place beside a transformer's
feed-forward block and identify as its analogue: a map into $\nglob$ features, a softmax
nonlinearity in place of a \textsc{relu}, and a map back through the transpose of the same
matrix. The source writes this operator without a message weight; we carry $\lamG$ because every other variable in the model carries one, and $\lamG = 1$ recovers
the source's form exactly.

\paragraph{Under the exact readout the head reaches the prediction only as a prior.}
This is the consequence \Cref{sec:variants} only gestures at. Folding $\Gv{t}$ into the sum--product of \Cref{prop:exact-readout} adds a factor
$\sum_k e^{\Bglob_{k,a}}$ to $\readout{t}(a)$ --- the form the source itself states --- so that
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-readout}
  \log \readout{t}(a) = \textstyle\sum_{c} \LSE_{j \in \Dep{t}} \Bfac{c}_{j,a}
    \;+\; \LSE_{k} \Bglob_{k,a}.
\end{equation}
%
The added term is a single $\nlab$-vector. It carries no index $t$ and no dependence on the
prefix, so whatever $\nglob$ is, only $\nlab$ numbers of the $\nglob\nlab$ in $\Bglob$ reach
the readout directly, and they reach it as a fixed per-label tilt of the mixture weights. It
does not cancel --- the readout log-sum-exps over labels rather than combining them linearly,
so an additive constant in $\log\readout{t}$ is not an additive constant in $\log\hat p$ ---
but it cannot carry context.

It would nevertheless be wrong to conclude from this that $\Gv{t}$ is inert, and the
distinction is what decides how the variant is run. The head also sits in the content stream, where
\Cref{eq:global-update} is evaluated at a position-dependent $\QZ{i}$ and reshapes every
$\qbar{i}$, and that path is present under either readout; what the exact readout removes is
the predictive inner loop, and with it the only place where the head acts on the slot being
predicted. A run in that mode would therefore add a constant tilt at the readout to an
indirect effect through the prefix and report the sum as the feed-forward analogue. Our
implementation refuses the combination rather than reporting that ablation; the constancy of
the direct term, and the fact that the end-to-end effect is not constant, are asserted
separately as validation checks (\Cref{app:validation}) instead of being assumed. The
configuration \Cref{sec:exp-ablations} measures is the mean-field one, in which the head acts
at the predicted slot as well as along the prefix.

\subsection{Serial update schedule}
\label{app:serial}

Two schedules are available and both are causal. The \emph{serial} schedule processes positions
left to right: at position $i$ it runs the two-block coordinate updates of
\Cref{eq:update-H,eq:update-Z} over $\big(\Zv{i}, \{\Hv{i}{c}\}_c\big)$ to convergence --- in
practice for a fixed number of inner rounds --- then freezes $\qbar{i}$ and advances. The \emph{layer-parallel} schedule computes
all $\QZ{i}^{(t)}$ from $\{\QZ{j}^{(t-1)}\}_{j<i}$ at once, under a single strict
lower-triangular mask, for $\niter$ iterations.

Each is the natural presentation of a different reading of the model. Serial is the clean
filtering story: every slot is a well-posed two-block mean-field problem on the per-step energy,
inheriting the per-step guarantees of the entropic Frank--Wolfe scheme from which the message
weights $\lamZ,\lamH$ come, and $\qbar{i}$ is frozen only after its own problem has been solved.
Layer-parallel is the relaxation in which the freezing happens one iteration at a time, and its
computation graph is exactly that of a depth-$\niter$, parameter-shared causal transformer.
Causality holds in both: in the serial schedule by induction on $i$, since the constants of slot
$i$'s problem are $\Sfac_{w_i,\cdot}$ and $\{\Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot}\}_{j<i}$ and no later quantity
appears anywhere in it; in the layer-parallel schedule because every path still runs from a
strictly earlier position under the mask.

All reported numbers use the layer-parallel schedule, for the reason that motivates the
baseline rather than for speed: the Looped transformer of \Cref{sec:experiments} is a shared
block applied $\niter$ times, so only under the layer-parallel schedule are the two models
compared at the same computation graph and the same effective depth, and only then does the
difference between them isolate structure.

The two schedules are not the same computation and we measure how far apart they land, on the
content stream, at $\nlab=32$, $\nchan=4$, $\krank=8$, $\clip=3$, $\niter=5$ and length $24$.
The mean total-variation distance between the label beliefs they produce is $3.4\times10^{-6}$
at initialisation scale $0.02$, rising to $0.111$ at $0.5$ and $0.682$ at $2.0$ --- where the
maximum reaches the largest value the metric admits, $1.000$, meaning that at some positions
the two schedules put their label mass on disjoint labels --- and falls back below $10^{-4}$
on an overfitted model. The reading is that the two agree in both degenerate regimes, near-uniform
beliefs at small parameter scale and near one-hot beliefs after fitting, and can disagree
completely in between. At the scales at which the model is initialised and at which it ends up,
the schedule is close to a free choice; at intermediate parameter scale it is not, which is why
we report it as a measured quantity rather than an implementation detail.

\subsection{Root node}
\label{app:root}

The root is not a variable in this model. The head domain is
$\Dep{t} = \{\textsc{root},1,\dots,t-1\}$, and $\rfac{c}$ is the \textsc{root} entry of the
contracted arc score of \Cref{eq:contract}: $\Bfac{c}_{\textsc{root},a} = \rfac{c}_a$, a learned
column of $\nlab$ numbers per channel, seeding the log-sum-exp of \Cref{eq:exact-readout} at
$j=\textsc{root}$. \Cref{sec:chain} says what it is for; what it stands in for is a
beginning-of-sequence token, since $\Dep{1} = \{\textsc{root}\}$ is a singleton and position
$1$ therefore has something to attend to without one being introduced.

\citet{wu2023probabilistic} add a root differently, in their \S2.3.5, and the differences are
worth stating because the two objects share a name and little else. Theirs is a genuine latent
variable $Z_{\textsc{root}}$ with its own label set, of a size they allow to differ from
$\nlab$, and its own ternary potential; its posterior is initialised uniform, updated during
inference like any other variable, and read afterwards as a sentence representation. It is also
optional: their model is well defined without it. Ours is a parameter, constant in the position
and in the input. It therefore carries no posterior and yields no sentence representation; it
cannot transmit information between positions, so it cannot threaten causality; it costs
$\nchan\nlab$ numbers rather than a $\nlab \times \nlab_{\textsc{root}} \times \nchan$ tensor;
and it is not optional, since without it the head domain of position $1$ would be empty.
Equivalently, $\rfac{c}$ is what their root variable collapses to when its distribution is held
fixed rather than inferred, by the same contraction of a fixed-distribution neighbour into a
binary factor that turns the frozen prefix into \Cref{eq:contract}. \NOTE{that last equivalence
is ours; \S2.3.5 does not state it, and the collapse argument it appeals to is the one Wu \& Tu
prove in their Appendix B.3.1 for the global feature variables.}

One property of the root column is measured rather than argued, and we report it because it
looks like a defect and is not. At initialisation $\rfac{c}$ reaches the attention in raw
$\nlab$-space, while the arc scores arrive contracted through
$\Bfac{c}_{j,a} = \E_{\qbar{j}}[\Tfac{c}_{a,\cdot}]$, which shrinks them by roughly
$\nlab^{-1/2}$ for a near-uniform prefix belief and again through the Kruskal product. Drawing
every parameter from the same $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ therefore leaves the \textsc{root} row
of $\Bfac{c}$ with a norm $121\times$ that of the contracted prefix rows at $\nlab=384$,
$\nchan=16$, $\krank=64$. The imbalance is nearly invisible in the attention at initialisation,
because a near-uniform $\QZ{t}$ averages the zero-mean entries of $\rfac{c}$ away; it becomes
visible only once the belief sharpens, at which point $\langle \QZ{t},\rfac{c}\rangle \to
\rfac{c}_a$ while $\langle \QZ{t},\Bfac{c}_{j,\cdot}\rangle \to \Bfac{c}_{j,a}$. What that
predicts --- an attention sink nobody chose --- does not happen. The measured \textsc{root}
attention mass at the last slot is $1.10\times$ uniform at the configuration above, untrained,
and $0.15\times$ uniform on an overfitted model: after training the model \emph{avoids} the
root rather than sinking into it. We expose the initialisation scale of $\rfac{c}$ as its own
hyperparameter, log the \textsc{root} mass at every iteration, and leave the default equal to
the other parameters', since changing it is a modelling decision and not a repair.
\NOTE{the run log records the trained figure both as a ratio, $0.15\times$ uniform, and as a
raw pair ($0.0001$ against a uniform $0.125$) that reduces to $0.0008\times$ instead; the ratio
is what the report and the project status carry, and the pair should be re-derived before this
sentence is fixed. Both readings are far below uniform, so which one is right changes the
figure but not the finding.}

\subsection{Factored labels}
\label{app:factored}

The widening of \Cref{sec:variants} replaces the single label variable at each position by
$\ncomp$ of them. Write $\nlab$ for the total label width, as everywhere else in this paper, so
that each component ranges over $\nlabc = \nlab/\ncomp$ values; the source writes $D$ for this
total. Position $t$ then carries $\Zv{t}^{(1)},\dots,\Zv{t}^{(\ncomp)}$ and $\ncomp$
word--label factors sharing the one word variable,
$\phi\big(\Wv{t},\Zv{t}^{(k)}\big) = \exp\big(\Sfac^{(k)}_{\Wv{t},\Zv{t}^{(k)}}\big)$, and each
head channel $c$ is assigned a child component $k(c)$, whose belief queries the channel and
receives its message, and a head component $k'(c)$, whose prefix belief is contracted into the
channel's keys:
$\Bfac{c}_{j,a} = \sum_b \qbar{j}^{(k'(c))}(b)\,\Tfac{c}_{a,b}$. The per-slot energy of
\Cref{eq:slot-energy} becomes the obvious sum,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factored-energy}
\begin{aligned}
  E_t = &-\textstyle\sum_w \QW{t}(w)\,\bfac_w \\
        &-\textstyle\sum_k \sum_{w,a} \QW{t}(w)\,\QZ{t}^{(k)}(a)\,\Sfac^{(k)}_{w,a}\\
        &-\textstyle\sum_c \sum_{j\in\Dep{t}} \sum_a \QH{t}{c}(j)\,\QZ{t}^{(k(c))}(a)\,
           \Bfac{c}_{j,a},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
multilinear as before, so every variable still has an honest gradient and the updates are still
softmaxes of it. The source states three checks and they survive reading: causality is
untouched, since nothing introduced here sees the future; the attention correspondence holds
per channel, with component-specific queries and keys; and the cost is linear, in the sense
that $\ncomp$ components of width $\nlabc$ cost between them what one component of width
$\nlab$ costs, each channel touching exactly one of them.

What factoring buys is a readout. The slot graph is still a tree --- $\Wv{t}$ joined to
$\ncomp$ stars, one per component --- so sum--product is still exact, and
\Cref{eq:exact-readout} becomes a \emph{product} of $\ncomp$ mixtures,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factored-readout}
\begin{aligned}
  \hat p(w) &\propto e^{\bfac_w}\prod_{k=1}^{\ncomp}
     \sum_{a=1}^{\nlabc} e^{\Sfac^{(k)}_{w,a}}\,\readout{t}^{(k)}(a),\\
  \log \readout{t}^{(k)}(a) &= \sum_{c\,:\,k(c)=k} \LSE_{j\in\Dep{t}} \Bfac{c}_{j,a},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
a strictly richer family than the single mixture at the same total width, and the strongest
graph-faithful head available in this model class. The interpretability story changes with it:
a position carries $\ncomp$ simultaneous discrete annotations, a feature bundle rather than one
monolithic tag, and channels are typed by which component they read.

The source's own caveat is the sharp part and we state it as it states it: under the mean-field
readout, factoring buys nothing over a flat label of width $\nlab$. The logits there are
$\bfac + \sum_k \Sfac^{(k)}q^{(k)}$, affine in the concatenated beliefs and hence of affine
dimension at most $\nlab$, which is exactly the bound \Cref{prop:readout}(i) gives for
$\ncomp=1$. Any gain therefore exists only through \Cref{eq:factored-readout}. The prediction
is about the head and not about the model as a whole: factoring also changes the inference
dynamics, since each channel now queries and keys off single components, and it changes the
parameter count, as the correction below shows. What \Cref{sec:exp-factored} tests is
accordingly the interaction --- an effect of $\ncomp$ present under \Cref{eq:factored-readout}
and absent under the mean-field readout is the one the source predicts, and an effect of the
same size under both is not.

Two things the source leaves open we had to settle. It does not say how channels are
assigned to components, noting only that per-channel labels
are the special case $\ncomp = \nchan$, $k(c) = c$, and that there is no reason to couple
$\ncomp$ to $\nchan$. We use $k(c) = c \bmod \ncomp$ and $k'(c) = \lfloor c/\ncomp\rfloor \bmod
\ncomp$, so that at $\nchan = \ncomp^2$ every ordered pair of components occurs exactly once and
no pair is privileged, and keep $k'(c) = k(c)$ --- components that never read each other --- as
the ablation that separates ``more mixture components'' from ``components that communicate''.
Second, the source introduces the global head of \Cref{app:globals} and the factored labels in
the same section but never says which component $\Gv{t}$ would hang off, or whether there is one
per component; our implementation refuses that combination rather than guessing it.

\paragraph{A correction to the parameter count.}
The source gives the parameter count under factoring as
$|\Vocab|\,\ncomp\,\nlabc = |\Vocab|\,\nlab$, ``identical to a flat label of width $\nlab$''.
That count is the word--label factors, and of them it is exactly true --- they are also the
overwhelming majority of the budget, and $\Sfac$ remains one tensor, read as $\ncomp$
contiguous blocks of width $\nlabc$ rather than split into $\ncomp$ separate matrices. Read as
a statement about the model's parameters, which is how the one-line version in
\Cref{sec:variants} takes it, it is false, because the arc factors do not follow. They become
per-component --- $\Tfac{c}$ acts $\nlabc \times \nlabc$ rather than $\nlab \times \nlab$ ---
so the non-embedding block, which is
$2(\clip{+}1)\nchan\,\nlabc\,\krank$ for the arc scores under the Kruskal decomposition plus
$\nchan\,\nlabc$ for the root column, \emph{falls} with $\ncomp$: as $1/\ncomp^2$ when the rank
is scaled with the component width, and as $1/\ncomp$ when the rank is held fixed. Measured at
$\nchan=4$, $\clip=3$ and $\krank=\nlabc$, the non-embedding counts are $32{,}896$ at
$(\nlab,\ncomp) = (32,1)$, $8{,}256$ at $(32,2)$, $32{,}896$ at $(64,2)$, $8{,}256$ at $(64,4)$
and $32{,}896$ at $(128,4)$. The consequence is the one worth carrying into the experiment:
factoring never buys its effect with parameters, and at a fixed non-embedding budget it buys
total label width --- the three equal counts in that list are three models of the same
non-embedding size at $\nlab = 32$, $64$ and $128$. \NOTE{this paragraph corrects \S22.2 of the
source document, whose count names only the word--label factors while the sentence around it
reads as a claim about the model; \Cref{sec:variants} inherits the unrestricted form and needs
the same restriction. The five counts are measured from the implementation, not read from the
source.}
