\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Wu & Tu's Conclusion is one short paragraph restating model and headline result, with no new
% material. Match that -- roughly 0.2 of a column. Written last, to the measured result.

We have derived the autoregressive counterpart of the probabilistic transformer. Causality, a
single global energy and contextual dependence cannot hold together, so we replace the single
field by a directed chain of conditional random fields in which the prefix enters each step as a
frozen condition and the anti-causal term is never formed; and we obtain a language-model head
by unclamping a variable the model already had, which makes tied embeddings, the output bias
and a mixture-of-softmaxes readout consequences of the construction rather than additions to it.
Empirically the model trains, reaches between $\MBMinRatioGpt$ and $\MBMaxRatioGpt$ times a
causal transformer's perplexity at its own parameter count --- the gap smallest at the smallest
budgets, and shallower in slope than either baseline on every range where the two can be
compared --- and learns label variables that carry recognisable syntactic categories
without supervision --- though whether its head variables learn attachment or collapse onto the
previous word turns out to depend on the same temperature the scaling does. Two things we did
not expect are the ones we would most want a successor to know: the readout the construction
itself distinguishes trains a hundred perplexity worse than the mean-field approximation to it,
and additional mean-field iterations only cost, so the best version of the model here runs one.
Both say that what limits it is not what the family can express but what this objective reaches
inside it. We hope this extends the probabilistic account of contextual representation
from analysis to generation, and that saying where it fails is as useful as saying where it
holds.
